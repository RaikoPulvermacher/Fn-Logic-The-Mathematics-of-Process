## 1. The Euler Paradox: Apparent Conservation
In classical mechanics (Euler-Logic), the fall distance is calculated as $s = 0.5 \cdot g \cdot t^2$.
At $t = 5\,\text{s}$ this yields approximately **122.625 m**.

**The problem of energy conservation:**
To arrive at this value, Euler-Logic must perform squarings ($t^2$) and roundings. Mathematically, this produces "remainders" (decimal places) that must be dissipated in physical reality as **resistance heat (approx. 89% efficiency loss)**.
> **Critique:** A system that requires "waste heat" to justify its computational errors violates the law of conservation of energy at the logical level.
